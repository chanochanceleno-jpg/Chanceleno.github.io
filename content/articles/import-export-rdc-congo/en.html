The DRC and the Republic of Congo are neighbours, but they do not work the same way: different currencies, different ports, different documents. Whether you are buying for Kinshasa or Lubumbashi, or selling from Pointe-Noire or Brazzaville, here is what you need to import and export without surprises.

Two Congos, four key cities

Kinshasa, capital of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, is the largest consumer market in the region. Most goods arrive through the port of Matadi on the Congo River, then travel on to the capital by road or rail.

Lubumbashi, in the south-east of the DRC, is the centre of the copper and cobalt mining region. Goods often arrive through East and Southern African ports such as Dar es Salaam or Durban, then by road through Zambia. The Lobito corridor, along the Benguela Railway, also links the region to the Atlantic through Angola.

Pointe-Noire is the deep-water port of the Republic of Congo and its oil-driven economic capital. Brazzaville, the political capital, faces Kinshasa across the river and is linked to Pointe-Noire by road and rail.

Currencies and payments: USD, CDF, XAF

  • DRC: the US dollar (USD) is used for most commercial transactions, the Congolese franc (CDF) for everyday payments. A USD offer is usually the simplest for a Congolese buyer.
  • Republic of Congo: the currency is the Central African CFA franc (XAF), pegged to the euro. An offer in EUR or XAF therefore limits currency risk.

Our offers can be issued in EUR, USD, CDF or XAF, depending on the operation. Payment is staged: a deposit limited to what is needed to place the order, and the balance against the shipping documents. CHANCELENO is not a payment institution and does not hold funds in escrow.

Documents and customs clearance

Both countries require electronic cargo tracking documents for sea shipments, such as the FERI in the DRC and the BESC in Congo, in addition to the invoice, packing list and bill of lading. The rules change regularly: have each shipment confirmed by your freight forwarder or customs broker before the goods leave. One missing document can hold a container at the port for weeks.

Exporting from the two Congos

We also support companies in the DRC and Congo that want to sell abroad: finding buyers and distributors, commercial and document preparation, certificates of origin and the rules of the destination country. Every export project is assessed case by case.
