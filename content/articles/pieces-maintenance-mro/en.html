A pump, motor or compressor down, and the whole production line waits. When the spare part comes from Europe or Asia, every mistake on the reference or the lead time costs days of downtime. Five habits greatly reduce this risk.

1.Photograph the nameplate

The equipment nameplate shows the manufacturer, model, serial number and often the year. A sharp photo, with the part number if it is legible, makes it possible to identify the right part the first time and avoids impossible returns.

2.List your critical parts before the breakdown

For each important piece of equipment, note the parts whose failure stops production: mechanical seals, bearings, belts, filters, circuit boards. This list, prepared calmly, saves precious time on the day you have to order urgently.

3.Compare the original part and equivalents

The original part (OEM) guarantees compatibility, but it can be expensive or slow to obtain. A quality equivalent may be suitable, provided its technical characteristics are checked and any difference from the original is stated in writing in the offer.

4.Keep a realistic safety stock

For critical parts with long lead times, a small stock on site often costs less than a week of downtime. Also group your routine needs into the same shipment: you reduce the transport cost per part.

5.Set lead time, Incoterm and inspection in the offer

A serious offer states the price, the real manufacturing or availability lead time, the Incoterm and the inspection planned before shipment. Without these, the announced lead time commits no one.
