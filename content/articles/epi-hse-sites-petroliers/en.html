On an oil, mining or industrial site, non-compliant protective equipment is not just a quality issue: it puts teams at risk and can be refused at the site gate. Buying internationally often gives better control over prices and lead times, provided you check six points before paying.

1.Start from the required standard, not the catalogue

Ask the site's HSE department for the list of standards expected for each item. In Europe, common references include EN ISO 20345 for safety footwear, EN 397 for helmets, EN 388 for gloves against mechanical risks, EN ISO 20471 for high-visibility clothing and EN ISO 11612 for clothing protecting against heat and flame. The supplier must meet your requirement, not the other way round.

2.Ask for certificates, not just markings

A printed logo proves nothing. Request the declaration of conformity, the type-examination certificate where required and the test reports, then check that the references match the ordered items exactly.

3.Take care with explosive atmospheres

Lamps, radios, detectors or tools used in explosive areas must be certified for that use, for example under the European ATEX directive or the international IECEx scheme. Standard equipment, however good, cannot replace them.

4.Prepare sizes and quantities per position

PPE orders often fail on sizes. Draw up a table per team and per position (shoe, clothing and glove sizes), with a margin for replacements. This avoids a costly second shipment.

5.Check the supplier and batch traceability

Verify the supplier's legal existence, its export experience and its ability to provide batch numbers. If a batch is defective, you must be able to identify and replace the affected items quickly.

6.Plan lead times and import documents

Include manufacturing, transport and customs clearance in your schedule, especially before a campaign or a shutdown. Prepare the documents required on arrival with your licensed customs broker, in particular the certificates of conformity.
