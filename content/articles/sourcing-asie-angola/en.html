More and more Angolan companies buy in Asia: machinery, truck parts, electrical equipment, solar panels, building materials. Prices are attractive, but distance, language and a huge spread in quality make every purchase a risk. Here is how to buy in China, India and Thailand without paying blind.

China, India, Thailand: what each country offers

China. The world's largest industrial base covers almost everything: machinery and equipment, electrical goods, solar, tools, building materials and parts for the Chinese trucks on Angolan roads, such as Sinotruk (HOWO), Shacman or FAW. The choice is vast and quality ranges from excellent to unacceptable, sometimes from the same supplier.

India. Strong in automotive and heavy-vehicle parts, pumps, valves, electrical equipment, textiles, generic medicines and medical supplies. English as the language of business makes it easier to exchange technical specifications and documents.

Thailand. A major automotive hub in South-East Asia, especially for pick-ups and light commercial vehicles, with a network of parts makers supplying the Japanese manufacturers. It is also a large food exporter, rice for example.

The right country depends on the product, the quality required, the lead time and the budget. Comparing an Asian offer with a European or Turkish one is often the best way to tell whether a price is fair.

The most common risks

  • The trader posing as a factory. Many trading companies present themselves as manufacturers. That is not necessarily bad, but it changes the price, the lead times and your control over production.
  • The perfect sample, the different production run. A good sample does not guarantee a good order. Without a pre-shipment check, you only find the difference in Luanda.
  • The change of bank details. An email announcing a new account for payment is a classic fraud warning sign. Always confirm through another channel.
  • The misunderstood Incoterm. An EXW or FOB price leaves transport, insurance and part of the costs to you. A low price can become much more expensive on arrival.
  • Certificates. Some products require specific standards and certificates. Ask for them before ordering, not after.
