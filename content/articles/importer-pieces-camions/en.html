Buying parts in Europe can cut costs and reduce fleet downtime. But a payment made to an unknown supplier thousands of kilometres away can turn into a total loss. These six steps reduce that risk.

1.Define the part precisely

Send the manufacturer's part number (OEM), the chassis number and photos of the used part. A wrong part number is the most common cause of returns that cannot be made.

2.Check the supplier before any payment

Confirm the company's business registration, its actual address and its export experience. A good price on an unknown website is no guarantee.

3.Insist on a firm offer

The offer must state the price, the delivery time and the Incoterm: for example FOB (up to the port of loading) or CIF (with freight and insurance to Luanda). Without an Incoterm, nobody knows who pays for what.

4.Pay in stages

Standard practice is a deposit to launch the order, then the balance against the shipping documents. Be wary of anyone who demands 100% before providing any document.

5.Inspect before shipment

Ask for photos, videos and a check of conformity and packaging before paying the balance. Once the goods have left, any mistake costs far more.

6.Prepare for arrival in Angola

Plan ahead for the documents required for import and confirm each step with your licensed customs broker, so the goods are not held at the port.
